# Product management question bank

Common PM interview questions by type, with what the interviewer is testing. Load this for
product roles only; other families build their list with `building-a-question-bank.md`, which
also holds the core openers every loop asks.

This bank is generic. Add 2 to 3 questions specific to the company from the research brief, and
pick the 12 to 20 most likely for this loop. Every behavioral answer uses one of the user's own
stories; never answer from an invented example.

---

## Behavioral

What they're testing: leadership, working with stakeholders, operating in ambiguity, influence
across functions, learning from mistakes.

### Leadership and influence
- "Tell me about a time you had to influence without authority."
- "Describe a time you pushed back on a stakeholder or a leader. What happened?"
- "Tell me about a time you got several teams with competing priorities to agree."
- "Give me an example of a hard product decision you made with incomplete data."
- "Tell me about a time you changed someone's mind. How?"

### Conflict and disagreement
- "Tell me about a time you disagreed with your manager. How did you handle it?"
- "Describe a conflict with an engineering partner. How did you resolve it?"
- "A stakeholder pushed back hard on your roadmap. What did you do?"

### Cross-functional execution
- "Walk me through a launch you led from start to finish."
- "Tell me about a time you coordinated five or more teams. What was hardest?"
- "Give me an example of trading speed against quality."
- "Your launch is two weeks out and engineering says it will slip a month. What do you do?"

### Failure and learning
- "Tell me about a product that failed or underperformed. What would you do differently?"
- "Describe a decision you made that turned out to be wrong. What happened next?"
- "Tell me about a mistake of yours that affected the team."

### Ambiguity and structure
- "Tell me about a vague problem with no playbook. How did you approach it?"
- "Describe how you set up a new area from scratch."
- "How do you operate when requirements keep changing?"

### Motivation and PM identity
- "Why product management?" (common for career changers)
- "What kind of PM are you? What are you unusually good at?"
- "Describe your product philosophy in a few sentences."
- "What does great product work look like to you?"

## Product sense and design

What they're testing: empathy for users, framing the problem before the solution, structured
thinking, taste.

### Improve a product
- "How would you improve <a product this company makes>?"
- "Pick a product you use every day. How would you make it 10x better?"
- "What's broken about a tool your team uses daily? How would you fix it?"

### Design for a user
- "Design a product for <older adults / commuters / remote workers / small-business owners>."
- "Design an alarm clock for people who are blind or have low vision."
- "Design a feature that helps new users of <product> get value in their first week."

### Favorite product
- "What's your favorite product and why?"
- "What product do you wish you had built?"
- "What's an underrated product you use?"

### Feature prioritization
- "We're building <X>. How would you pick the first three features?"
- "You have three engineers for six weeks. What do you build?"
- "How do you decide what not to build?"

## Metrics and analytics

What they're testing: defining success, choosing one metric that matters, comfort with data,
reading patterns in numbers.

### Define success
- "How would you measure the success of <feature or product>?"
- "What's the North Star metric for <product>? Why?"
- "If you could track only one metric for <product>, which would it be?"
- "How do you define a successful launch?"

### Investigate a drop
- "Daily active users dropped 20% last week. Walk me through how you'd investigate."
- "Mobile conversion fell 15%. How do you approach it?"
- "Revenue is flat but we're acquiring more users. What's going on?"
- "Satisfaction scores dropped 10 points after the last release. What do you do?"

### Set up measurement
- "We just launched <feature>. What would you track in the first 30 days?"
- "How do you know an experiment is ready to ship?"
- "What's the difference between a leading and a lagging indicator? Give an example."

### Experimentation
- "How would you design an A/B test for <feature>?"
- "An experiment shows +2% click-through but −1% conversion. Do you ship it?"
- "The result is statistically significant but tiny. What do you do?"

## Estimation and market sizing

What they're testing: breaking a big number into parts, reasonable assumptions stated out loud,
staying calm with no data.

### Market sizing
- "How many rideshare trips happen per day in a city of one million people?"
- "Estimate the market size for <product category>."
- "How many people in the US use a fitness tracker?"
- "What's the addressable market for <this company's product>?"

### Back of the envelope
- "How much new storage does a large email service need per day?"
- "How many piano tuners work in a city of three million?"
- "If 1% of US adults adopt <product> at $10 a month, what's the annual revenue?"

Approach: segment → estimate each segment → add up → sanity-check. Say every assumption out loud.

## Strategy

What they're testing: market thinking, awareness of competitors, build vs. buy vs. partner,
long-range judgment.

### Market and competition
- "Should <company> enter <new market>? How would you decide?"
- "A competitor just shipped the feature you've been building. What do you do?"
- "How would you decide between building, buying or partnering for <capability>?"
- "What's the biggest threat to <company>'s business in the next three years?"

### Tradeoffs
- "You have three product bets on the table. How do you choose?"
- "How do you balance short-term revenue against long-term user trust?"
- "The CEO wants to ship a feature that design says will hurt the experience. What do you do?"

### Vision and roadmap
- "Where is <product category> headed in five years?"
- "What would you put on a 12-month roadmap for <product>?"
- "If you were the PM for <product> tomorrow, what's the first thing you'd change?"

## AI products (when the role touches AI features)

What they're testing: fluency with how AI features behave, managing outputs that aren't
deterministic, user trust, responsible shipping.

### Building AI features
- "How is managing an AI product different from managing traditional software?"
- "How do you define success for a feature whose outputs vary from run to run?"
- "How do you keep user trust when the AI gets something wrong?"
- "When should a feature use AI, and when are plain rules better?"
- "How do you design AI features for people who aren't technical?"

### Adoption
- "What stops companies from adopting AI tools, and how would you address it?"
- "How do you design for users who don't trust AI yet?"
- "People are ignoring an AI feature we shipped. How would you increase usage?"

### Safety and quality
- "How do you weigh capability against safety when shipping an AI feature?"
- "What checks would you put in place before an AI feature reaches millions of users?"
- "The model produced something harmful in production. What do you do in the first hour?"
- "How would you know an AI feature is getting better or worse over time?"

## Leadership and management (lead, principal or group PM roles)

- "How do you grow and develop junior PMs?"
- "Tell me about hard feedback you gave someone on your team."
- "How do you get a large cross-functional team behind one direction?"
- "What does good product culture look like to you?"
- "How do you join a team that already has momentum?"

## Why this company (always comes up)

- "Why do you want to work here?"
- "Why now? Why is this the right time for you?"
- "What do you know about us that most candidates don't?"
- "What would you do in your first 30, 60 and 90 days?"
- "What's a product decision we made that you think was right, or wrong?"

Build these from the research brief and the family's *Why me* line in `profile/positioning.md`:
their problem, the user's matching experience, and one specific, true reason for now. Never
generic praise.
