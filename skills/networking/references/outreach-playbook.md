# Outreach playbook: structures and hard rules

Generic craft for professional outreach. The *content* (who the user is, their numbers, their
stories) comes from `profile/me.md` and `profile/evidence.md`; the *voice* (greetings, sign-offs,
rhythm, banned words) comes from `profile/voice.md`. Where `profile/rules.md` or `profile/voice.md`
disagrees with this file, the user's file wins.

All examples below are fictional (Jordan Lee, Acme Corp, Northwind, Globex). Use them for shape,
length and order, never for wording to copy.

---

## Hard rules

1. **No jargon.** Plain language. If it reads like a LinkedIn post or a job description, rewrite it.
2. **Be brief.** A cold email fits **one phone screen without scrolling**: ~150 words, 3 short
   paragraphs max. Texts and DMs: 1 to 1.5 paragraphs. If a sentence is not load-bearing, cut it.
3. **Credentials as 2 to 3 bullets, not prose,** when the message carries background. Each bullet
   is one credential plus one specific number from the evidence ledger.
4. **Lead with the ask** in emails and DMs. State what you want, then give context. Cover letters
   are the exception.
5. **No cringe phrases.** Never "pick your brain" or "compare notes".
6. **No generic openers.** "Hope this message finds you well" gets deleted. Start with something
   real.
7. **Do not manufacture proof of attention.** Never write "I've been following your work" or "I've
   watched X unfold" unless it is literally true. Use the actual reason the user is writing, even
   if it is small (a friend mentioned the company, plus one recent public signal). The smaller true
   version is stronger than the bigger fake one.
8. **Frame product observations as curiosity about strategy, not gaps you spotted.** To a senior
   stranger, "I'm curious how the team thinks about X" reads as peer engagement; "Curious why X
   isn't solved" reads as a critique. Same observation, different posture.
9. **No repeated key words in one short blurb.** If a 4-sentence blurb uses the same load-bearing
   noun or verb twice, swap one so each sentence pulls weight.
10. **No rhetorical claims you cannot defend.** "The gap no one is closing" or "companies least
    equipped to do X" sounds punchy but judges other people or companies. A reader who knows the
    space will challenge it. Use defensible specifics.
11. **Never presume the recipient's situation.** No "right in your wheelhouse". State the user's
    facts and the company's public facts; let the reader connect them.
12. **Do not outsource agency to a mutual connection.** Avoid "X is flagging my application" or "X
    said you'd push me through". Write "X shared the role and suggested I reach out" and let the
    reader infer the favor.
13. **Cause-then-effect order for warm intros.** "Riley shared the role and suggested I reach out"
    beats "Riley suggested I reach out after sharing the role".
14. **Describe the user's current status only the way `profile/me.md` says to.** If it says
    nothing, state what they did and ask before adding any status line.

## Structure by format

### LinkedIn connection note (300-character hard cap)

- What fits: one hook (shared school, employer, or event), one credential line, one line on current
  work, a short close. What does not: an "I applied" line *and* full credentials. Credentials win;
  the application speaks for itself.
- **Strip `https://` from links.** LinkedIn linkifies a bare domain, which saves characters.
- **Count characters before sending**; do not eyeball it.
- **If the post said "reach out if interested," answer that.** "Applying this week." answers the
  invitation and asks for nothing.
- **If a referral is already in flight, ask for nothing,** and do not name the referrer before they
  have confirmed.

### LinkedIn DM (cold)
- Open: acknowledge the cold message, or a specific hook about them
- Bridge: 1 sentence on the user's relevant background, connected to their world
- Close: a real question, not a meeting request
- Length: 4 to 6 sentences

### LinkedIn DM (warm, shared connection)
- Lead with the connection immediately; 2 to 3 sentences total

### LinkedIn DM variants by recipient
- **Peer-level PM/engineer/designer on the target team:** one paragraph, ~55 words. Hook → reason
  for writing → one-sentence background that ladders to their team → low-pressure ask.
- **Senior leader, no connection:** "get on radar" note. No meeting ask, no question. Name, the role
  applied for, 2 sentences of background that show *scope*, a link, and a one-line close in the user's own words.
- **Head of function, redirect ask:** do not ask for their time; ask who they would suggest the user
  talk to. Lower friction, and lets them volunteer themselves.
- **Recruiter:** asking for 15 minutes is fine (recruiters expect it). Make the ask specific (how
  the team is organized, what it is hiring for next), not "learn more about the role".

### Email (cold or warm)
- **Ask first**: the concrete request (a short call, one question, an intro) in the first lines
- Then 1 to 2 sentences of relevant background, connected to their world
- Then 1 genuine question that shows homework
- Max 3 paragraphs, readable on a phone
- For senior leaders, a one-sentence identity line can precede the ask in the same paragraph
- Where pleasantries go (top, end, or nowhere) follows `profile/voice.md`

### Intro request (asking a mutual)
- Ask first: name the person and the introduction you want in the first sentence
- 1 to 2 sentences on why this person and what the user wants to discuss
- The mutual does not need the full backstory

### Subject lines (cold to founders or senior leaders)
- Lead with the user's role (fast parse)
- Include one scarce credential, not a commodity claim anyone could make
- Name the company
- Strong: "Growth analyst who cut churn 18% at Northwind, re: Acme data team"
- Weak: "Hard-working generalist exploring Acme" (nothing scarce)

### Cover letter
- The one format that does not lead with the ask. Length, structure and checklist live in one
  place: `skills/resume/references/cover-letter.md`. Follow it.

### Third-person referral blurb (to be forwarded)
- **Always third person.** It is written for the referrer to paste.
- **No meeting ask inside the blurb.** The ask lives in the wrapper email to the referrer.
- Template, one sentence each:
  1. **Who they are:** a positioning line in the target's domain ("Jordan Lee is a pricing analyst who
     runs experiments on subscription plans.")
  2. **Most recently:** title, company, the one thing they owned, in the reader's nouns (not
     internal project names)
  3. **One proof number** in its own sentence
  4. **On the side** (optional): a built artifact, described by what someone can do with it
  5. **What they want at this company:** name the team and tie to what it is building
- Use the user's correct title (check `profile/me.md`); split an overloaded sentence rather than
  chaining unlike items.

### Referral-ask wrapper (email to the referrer)
- Short and warm. Thank them, name the req ID and link the posting, attach the CV, and ask plainly:
  "Could you help with a referral, or let me know the best next step?"
- Put the blurb below a divider (e.g. `///`) so it is easy to forward.

## Fictional examples

### Cold email, ask first (~110 words)

> **Subject:** Growth analyst, question on Acme's pricing team
>
> Hi Priya,
>
> Do you have 20 minutes in the next couple of weeks? I applied for the Pricing Analyst role on
> Monday and have one question about how the team picks which tests to run first.
>
> Some background:
> - Analyst at Northwind, where I rebuilt the discount model and cut margin leakage 12%
> - Ran 40+ pricing tests across three product lines
>
> I'm curious how Acme thinks about testing price against bundling now that the new plans are out.
>
> Cheers,
> Jordan

### Cold DM, proof-of-work lead

> Hi Riley, I'm writing cold, so feel free to ignore this. I put in an application for the Support
> Operations role at Globex last week.
>
> I built a small analysis of 900 public reviews of Globex's help center; the top complaint is
> tickets closed before the fix ships. In case it's useful: example.com/globex-support
>
> For background, I run support ops at Northwind for a 60-person team. Happy to share the raw data.

### Third-person referral blurb

> Jordan Lee is a pricing analyst who runs experiments on subscription plans. Most recently, Jordan
> owned the discount model at Northwind across three product lines. Jordan's tests cut margin
> leakage 12%. On the side, Jordan built a public price-tracking tool that shows how subscription
> prices change over time. Jordan is excited to help Acme's pricing team test new plans faster.

## Format quick reference

| Format | Length | Structure |
|--------|--------|-----------|
| Connection note | 300 chars | Hook → credential → close |
| LinkedIn DM (cold) | 4 to 6 sentences | Hook → 1-sentence background → real question |
| LinkedIn DM (warm) | 2 to 3 sentences | Connection → brief ask |
| Email | 3 short paragraphs | **Ask first** → background → 1 real question |
| Intro request | 2 to 3 short paragraphs | **Ask first** → why this person → personal close |
| Cover letter | see `skills/resume/references/cover-letter.md` | see that file |
| Referral blurb | 4 to 5 sentences | Who → most recently → proof → side build → why this company |

Sign-offs come from `profile/voice.md`.
